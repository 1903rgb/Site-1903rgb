The brief

A converted barn with a field for a garden.

Harriet and James bought a converted stone barn with an old paddock on a south-west slope. The ground fell 2.1 m from the house to the boundary, and there was no shelter from the wind off the wold.

They wanted roses in June, somewhere to eat outside, and fruit. They also asked for a garden that would not look empty from the kitchen in winter, which is where they spend most of their mornings.

Constraints on site
  • Fall across the plot2.1 m
  • Topsoil depth18–25 cm
  • Prevailing windSW, exposed
  • Conservation areaYes
  • Maintenance time4 hrs / week

1,840 plants, timed to take turns.

The timeline

August to April, then the first summer.

Stone work ran through the dry autumn. Bare-root hedging went in during January, while the plants were dormant.

  1. Aug 2024

    Survey and two concepts

    Levels taken every 2 m. Soil tested at six points: pH 7.6 to 8.0, free-draining brash.

  2. Sep 2024

    1:50 planting plan signed off

    1,840 plants specified. Fixed build price agreed at £148,000 including VAT.

  3. Oct – Dec 2024

    Walls, terrace and paths

    Two retaining walls built dry, 0.9 m and 1.2 m high, from 31 tonnes of stone quarried 11 miles away.

  4. Jan – Feb 2025

    Bare-root planting

    Beech hedge, roses and fruit trees planted dormant. Orchard meadow seeded at 4 g per m².

  5. Mar – Apr 2025

    Perennials and handover

    Pot-grown perennials in, 5 cm mulch over every bed. Handover walk with a written care calendar.

  6. Jun 2025 →

    First summer, fortnightly care

    Seven plants replaced under guarantee out of 1,840. Maintenance continues on a fortnightly contract.
